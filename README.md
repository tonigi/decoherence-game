# Born Rule

A wavefunction puzzle game. Each level gives you a potential landscape, an initial wavepacket and a target. You shape how ψ evolves, then pick the moment to measure. Waiting lets the packet travel *and* disperse, so the puzzle is arranging constructive interference over the target at the right time, and observing then.

Prototype scope: chapters 1-4 (free propagation, tunnelling, interference, bound states), chapter 5's region detectors (which-way information, Zeno freezing) and chapter 6's polarization optics (two-component ψ: polarizers, rotators, waveplates, Stern–Gerlach magnets, an interaction-free bomb test, a full-loop Stern–Gerlach interferometer), chapter 7's gratings and lattices (Kapitza–Dirac, Talbot, Bragg) chapter 8, where the plane is two particles on a line (Hong–Ou–Mandel), and chapter 9, where portal edges make the world a ring (Aharonov–Bohm flux, Bloch oscillations, flux-shifted momentum combs, the pair flux quantum h/2e, an edge switched into a drain, revivals, a velocity selector, a bound pair, the fermion parity effect). 36 levels, including a guided tutorial chapter 0 (First Light). Levels that re-enact a textbook experiment show its name (`experiment`) under the title, on the map card and in the physics drawer.

## Run

No build step, no dependencies (ES modules, so serve over HTTP):

```sh
npm start          # python3 -m http.server 8000  ->  http://localhost:8000
npm test           # physics + game-logic tests (node:test)
npm run verify     # every level: reference reaches 2 stars, naive play stays below 1 star
npm run solve -- levels/2-2.json   # random-restart optimiser over the setup parameters
```

### Publishing

`.github/workflows/pages.yml` runs the tests and `verify`, then publishes the static files to GitHub Pages on every push to `main` (or by hand from the Actions tab). One-time setup: repository Settings → Pages → Source: **GitHub Actions**.

## Controls

| | |
|---|---|
| Levels | the chapter map (title screen → Chapters, or the level button in the top bar), ◀ ▶ or `[` `]` for previous / next. Routes: `#title`, `#map`, `#<level id>` |
| Setup | drag the packet (position) and every editable property on the playfield: the arrow head (momentum k₀), the round handle on the dashed ring (width σ); select a placed item to get square handles for width / height / radius and a ◆ stalk above it for its strength (V, or a detector's measurement strength). Sliders mirror all of them. Add items from the inventory, drag them, `Del` removes |
| `Space` | play / pause (starts the run from setup) |
| `Enter` | **Observe**: sample |ψ|², collapse, win or lose. At `t_max` the world observes for you |
| click (while running) | **Kick**: momentum impulse toward the cursor, limited charges |
| arrows / WASD | **Field tilt**: uniform force while held |
| `1`-`9` | gates toggle; detectors fire (hold the key to pulse a "hold" detector repeatedly) |
| Branches | after a run with detector firings, the Branches panel lists the outcome histories (`Y4 N`, …) with their Born weights and P_T; click one to see its wave function |
| `R` | back to setup, same dice. `V` cycles view (domain colouring / |ψ|² / momentum |φ(k)|² / polarization on spinor levels), `C` current arrows, `G` ghost preview, `M` sound |
| `?` / `Esc` | controls and legends sheet / close panels. Speed, expectation mode, ghost and current live under Settings |

Win condition (hybrid): the live meter shows P_T = ∫_T |ψ|². The observation is a genuine Born-rule sample (P(hit) = P_T exactly); a hit wins, and **stars come from the skill score**, so skill is rewarded even when the dice are unkind. Without detectors the score is P_T at the moment of observation. If a detector fired, a single run's P_T is post-selected by the detector outcomes (a lucky "yes" can drop the packet onto the target), so the score is the **branch ledger** instead: P_T averaged over every detector outcome of your inputs (`src/ledger.js`). Mines are anti-targets. Probability absorbed at the boundary is a loss branch of the observation. "Expectation mode" wins iff the score ≥ the level's threshold, with no roll; it is fixed for the whole run and keeps its own stars.

## Interface

* **Title** (`#title`): a live, exact free Gaussian behind the title; Start goes to the tutorial chapter, Continue to the last level, Chapters to the map.
* **Chapter map** (`#map`): one row per chapter with its idea; level cards show the real initial state and geometry (rendered through `Session` + `Renderer` at 64², lazily) and your stars.
* **Playfield**: always fits the viewport (a square sized by the stage container, `cqw`/`cqh`), with the absorbing edge drawn as a hatched frame, a phase-wheel legend that follows the view, and a view switch. Under it the **transport bar** merges the clock and the P_T(t) timeline (ghost preview, this run, star lines, the observation window, a gold mark where the world observes on fixed-time levels) with Play and Observe always visible.
* **Rail**: the goal, a one-line **physics point** with "Why?", the probabilities (P_T as a ring with star ticks; mines, lost and momentum budget as bars; the Heisenberg / energy / polarization readout), the Branches panel, real-time tools, then launch sliders and inventory. After an observation the meters hold their values at the moment of observation.
* **Result card**: shown after a short pause on the crosshair and collapsed packet. Without detectors it shows the Born rule as an ensemble (100 dots, P_T·100 of them hits, yours outlined); with detectors it explains the branch-averaged score.
* **Physics drawer**: "Why?" opens the level's long explanation (`physics.learn`: the idea, the maths, things to try), beside the running field.
* **Tutorial** (`src/tutorial.js`): guided levels carry `tutorial` steps `{anchor, title, text, phys, wait}`. Each step spotlights one control and advances on what it waits for: `next`, a game signal (`play`, `observe`, `result`, `drag`) or a time (`t>=8`), so the player learns by doing. It runs the first time a guided level is opened; "Tutorial" in the top bar replays it.
* Level text keys: `goal` (what to do, shown in the rail), `physics` `{point, eq, learn: {idea, maths, try}}` (items are HTML strings or `{eq}` formula blocks), and the older `intro` (now the goal's tooltip and the fallback).

## Design notes (how this maps to the plan)

* **Solver.** 2D TDSE, ħ = m = 1, split-step Fourier with Strang splitting (V/2 → T → V/2), `src/sim.js`. Exactly unitary without the absorber (tested to 1e-10). Runs on the CPU in plain JS on a 128² grid (64² for the ghost preview and the solver); the structure maps directly onto a WebGPU port, which is the obvious next step for 256²/512² showcase levels.
* **Boundaries.** Smooth quadratic complex absorbing potential, 6 length units wide. Absorbed probability = 1 − norm, shown as "lost".
* **Momentum ceiling.** k_max = π/dx = 10 here. Levels keep launch speeds ≤ 0.6 k_max (checked by a test); a "momentum budget" meter measures the probability above 60 % of Nyquist and flags aliasing.
* **Smooth everything.** Potentials and projector masks use tanh edges of fixed physical width (`SOFTNESS` = 0.45 units at every resolution, so the 64² ghost and solver see the same barriers as the 128² game: tunnelling agrees to 3 %, which a test checks). Rects can be rotated (`angle`), which gives 45° beam splitters and mirrors. The tunnelling test compares the simulated transmission with an exact 1D integration through the *rasterised* barrier (3 % agreement).
* **Measurement.** Position: draw u ∈ [0,1); u ≥ norm means "already absorbed"; otherwise invert the cumulative |ψ|² and collapse by multiplying ψ by a Gaussian of width σ_m = 1 (not a delta) and renormalising. Detectors use Kraus operators M_yes = m, M_no = √(1−m²) with a smooth mask m, so M_yes² + M_no² = 1; a "no" is an interaction-free measurement that carves ψ out of the detector region. A test checks that P_T is a martingale under a detector firing.
* **Branch ledger.** After a run with detector firings, the run's open-loop inputs are replayed on 64², splitting every branch into M_yes ψ / M_no ψ (weights p, 1−p) at each firing; kicks, tilt and gates act on all branches alike (`Sim.step(n, others)` steps them in lock-step). One-shot detectors give an exact 2-branch average. Pulsed (Zeno) detectors double the tree on every pulse, so beyond 16 branches the ensemble is compressed to the 16 leading eigenvectors of its density matrix ρ = Σ w |ψ⟩⟨ψ| (via the small Gram matrix; everything downstream is linear in ρ, so this is the best rank-16 approximation and the discarded eigenvalue weight bounds the score's error, typically ~1 %). It is deterministic, matches the exact tree and Monte Carlo, and costs about 2 s for 48 pulses. Detectors may have a `strength` s < 1 (weak measurement: M_yes = √s·m, M_no = √(1 − s m²)), which the same machinery handles.
* **Polarization (spinor levels).** `"spinor": true` gives ψ a second component, ψ = (ψ_H, ψ_V): a photon-like polarization, or a spin-1/2 under the Poincaré ↔ Bloch map. Kinetic energy and the scalar potential act alike on both (two FFT pairs per step, ~2× the cost). Optics are a per-cell 2×2 term h₀ + h·σ (`src/optics.js`), which commutes with the scalar part (∝ 1), so each potential half-step multiplies by the exact matrix exponential exp(−i dt/2 (h₀ + h·σ)) on the sparse cells that carry optics; overlapping optics add before exponentiating. A **polarizer** at θ is the non-Hermitian projector −iW·P(θ+90°): the crossed component decays and counts as lost (W = 6 over 2 units: Malus's law to 0.3 %, 0.03 % leakage, ~1 % reflection); with `V` instead it is reflected (a polarizing beam splitter). A **rotator** is ω σ_y (polarization turns by ω per unit time inside, like a Larmor spin rotator). A **retarder** is (δ/2) n(θ)·σ, so its retardance is δ × dwell time and a half-wave plate is a thickness, w = πv/δ. A **Stern–Gerlach magnet** (`zeeman`) is g·v·n(θ)·σ with v the coordinate across it: the force ∓g splits the beam into the polarization along θ (up) and the crossed one (down), with Born weights cos² and sin². Any optic with `bomb: true` is a polarization-selective bomb: what it absorbs counts as caught (a mine, an observation's "boom" branch), exactly, like the scalar absorbing bombs. Detectors, the ledger (the Gram matrix sums both components), kicks and the Born sample all act on the full spinor. The polarization view colours by the local Stokes vector (hue = axis, white = circular), and the HUD shows the mean polarization.
* **Determinism.** A session is `(level, setup, seed, event log)`. All dice come from one seeded PRNG and all events are applied at integer step boundaries, so `replay()` reproduces a run exactly (`Copy replay JSON` on the result banner; `node tools/verify.js --replay file.json` re-verifies headlessly). The CPU build is a natural reference for verifying GPU-played leaderboard runs.
* **Level format.** `levels/*.json`: grid, `launch` ranges (`[value, min, max]` = editable), static `elements` (rect with optional `angle` / disc / slit wall / `harmonic` well {x, y, omega, R}, drawn as equipotential rings, optionally a toggleable `gate` (with `ramp` steps; a gated `harmonic` well is a trap you pulse, a gated `absorb` element an absorbing shutter whose loss counts as lost); `absorb: W` makes an element an absorbing bomb, whose swallowed probability counts as a mine), `detectors`, `inventory` (items, kick charges, tilt), `targets`, `mines`, star thresholds, a `reference` solution and a `naive` one (what ignoring the level's mechanic gives; observed at its best moment when it has no `observeAt`). `verify.js` requires reference ≥ 2 stars and naive < 1 star, so every level demonstrably rewards its lesson; `"naive": false` with a `naiveSkip` reason opts out. `minObserveTime` = `tmax` makes the observation time fixed. `"observe": "momentum"` makes the final measurement a momentum measurement: targets and mines are then regions of k-space (`x`, `y` = kx, ky), and `kView` zooms the momentum view. An item's `area` is a rect [x0, y0, x1, y1] or a list of them (placement zones: the item snaps into the nearest). Spinor levels (`"spinor": true`) may set `launch.pol` (deg) and `launch.ellip` (−1…1, ±1 circular); any element can carry `optic: {kind: "polarizer", angle, absorb | V}`, `{kind: "rotator", omega}` or `{kind: "retarder", angle, delta}`, also with a `gate` (closed = inserted); `{kind: "zeeman", angle, gradient}` and `bomb: true` on any of them; inventory items of kind `polarizer` (keys w, h, pol; `reflect`, `absorb`, `bomb`, `tilt` on the spec), `rotator` (w, h, omega), `retarder` (w, h, pol, delta) and `zeeman` (w, h, pol, gradient) are optics the player places, with `pol` the axis in degrees (a dial handle on the playfield); a target with `pol` sits behind an analyzer and scores |⟨pol|ψ⟩|². A target `{shape: "state", x, y, sigma}` is a quantum state instead of a region: the observation is the projector onto that coherent state, and P_T is the fidelity |⟨φ|ψ⟩|² (it must be the only target, without mines). A `lattice` element `{x, y, V, period, angle?, w?, h?}` is V sin²(πu/period) (minima at its centre), optionally inside a soft rect window, and an inventory item of kind `lattice` (w, h, V, period) places one. A `gate` with `pulse: n` fires an n-step pulse on each key press and reopens by itself (created closed, it fires at t = 0). `launch.sigmaT` gives the packet a different width across its motion, `field: [Fx, Fy]` adds a constant force, and `particles: "bosons" | "fermions"` reads the grid as ψ(x₁, x₂) of two particles on a line and (anti)symmetrises the launch under exchange. `experiment` is the textbook name of the experiment a level re-enacts, shown to the player. `boundary` is `"absorb"` (the default), `"periodic"` or `{x, y}` per axis: a periodic axis has no absorber, so its edges are portals (what leaves comes back on the other side, phase intact, drawn as dashed cyan lines); kicks along it come in whole quanta 2π/L. A field or tilt along a periodic axis acts as a flux growing in time (the kinetic momentum is k + F t; no sawtooth potential), and `launch.flux` (flux quanta, slider "flux Φ/Φ₀") threads the ring of axis `fluxAxis` (default x): a twist ψ(x + L) = e^{2πiΦ/Φ₀}ψ(x). The packet is launched with the same kinetic momentum whatever the flux. Momentum measurements, k-space targets, the momentum view and the HUD all use the kinetic momentum k + g (on a ring with a flux the comb of allowed momenta is shifted by a fraction of a cell; the momentum view draws ring levels' k cells crisp). `fluxAxis: "xy"` threads both axes (two particles on one ring). A k-space region may set `edge` (its softness; the default 0.08 is half a k cell, a sharp 0.005 resolves single teeth). `postselect: {min}` scores only what survives: P_T and P_M are divided by max(norm, min) and the observation samples among the survivors (the floor stops a selector that passes nothing from scoring; below it the shortfall is lost). A `pair` element `{omega, R}` is a harmonic bond ω²u²/2 between the two particles of a two-particle level, u = (x₁ − x₂)/√2 wrapped round the box, flat beyond |u| = R; it is drawn as contours along the diagonal. On a periodic axis the launch packet is summed over its periodic images, so a packet wider than the ring has no seam. `drains: [{side: "left" | "right" | "top" | "bottom", key, label}]` make an edge switchable between portal (open) and drain (closed: an absorbing strip, a smooth bump 2 sin²(πd/6) that also vanishes at the seam; what it swallows is lost).
* **Solver-assisted design.** `tools/solve.js` is a derivative-free optimiser (random restarts + (1+λ) search) over launch and inventory parameters. It was used to check that every level is solvable and to calibrate the star thresholds from the achievable P_T. The adjoint-gradient version is the planned upgrade (the dynamics are linear and differentiable), enabling hints and difficulty ratings by optimum sharpness.
* **Sound.** `src/audio.js` sonifies ψ with Web Audio. The grid is cut into 16 horizontal bands tuned to a just-intoned A major pentatonic (top = high); each band's voice has amplitude √P_band, so total power = Σ P = norm: unitary evolution keeps the loudness constant and absorption fades it. Direction is binaural (Web Audio HRTF panners, best on headphones): a band whose probability is concentrated in x is heard from the azimuth of its mean x (left edge = 90° left, right edge = 90° right); as its x spread grows (sd 6 % → 20 % of the width) it crossfades into a surround bed of four decorrelated sources around the listener, so a smeared or left/right-split ψ comes from all around rather than from a phantom centre. Bells come from their sampled x. RMS momentum opens the low-pass. The melody is the Born rule: each beat a cell is sampled from |ψ|² (a rest for the absorbed branch) and rung as a bell. P_T raises a high halo, P_M a slowly beating low drone; kicks, gates and detector clicks have their own chimes. On observation everything fades to silence, leaving one faint tone at the position where the particle was found. Long convolution reverb plus a feedback delay give the ethereal wash. The audio dice use `Math.random`, never the session PRNG, so replays are unaffected.
* **Ghost preview.** Optional low-res forward simulation of the current launch (no real-time actions) drawn as a faint P_T(t) curve.

## Levels

| | |
|---|---|
| 0-1 Meet ψ | guided: what ψ is, Play, the P_T meter, Observe, stars from P_T |
| 0-2 Look | guided: timing and the Born rule as odds; a mine behind the target punishes late looks |
| 0-3 Aim | guided: aim with the momentum arrow, the ghost preview, the physics drawer |
| 1-1 Rendezvous | measured at t = 10 sharp: speed is set by the distance, the width optimum is σ₀ = √(t/2) |
| 1-2 Between the Mines | aiming with a mine on each side; choosing when to observe |
| 1-3 Nudge | kicks and field tilt |
| 1-4 Sharp Momentum | a momentum measurement: σ_x·σ_k ≥ ½ wants a wide packet, the edges of the world want a narrow one |
| 2-1 Thin Wall | tunnelling vs energy |
| 2-2 Mirror | place a barrier; leakage goes to the mines |
| 3-1 Two Slits | fringes, mines on the dark fringes |
| 3-2 Mach–Zehnder | 45° splitters and mirrors; a phase plate swaps the bright and dark ports |
| 4-1 Coherent State | harmonic well: only σ₀ = 1/√(2ω) swings without breathing |
| 4-2 Fourier Lens | a quarter period swaps x and k: a wide packet focuses to 1/(2ωσ); finite aperture |
| 4-3 Delta-Kick Cooling | a timed harmonic pulse (gate) removes the x–k correlation of an expanding packet: 7 % → 80 % in a k = 0 target |
| 4-4 Bang-Bang | trap transport (shortcut to adiabaticity): the target is the ground state of trap B; a midpoint trap held for half a period π/ω parks the packet there at rest (fidelity 96 %, sudden switch 0 %) |
| 5-1 Which Way? | on the same interferometer, a which-path detector lights up the dark port |
| 5-2 Hold Still | Zeno freezing with a pulsed detector; the detector size has an interior optimum |
| 6-1 Three Polarizers | crossed polarizers pass nothing; inserting more lets light through (30°/60° in order: 42 % of the light, 25 % with one at 45°) |
| 6-2 Watched Pot | polarization Zeno effect: a rotator turns H into V, and N equally spaced polarizers keep it H with cos^{2(N+1)}(π/2(N+1)); spacing has an interior optimum |
| 6-3 Half-Wave | a lossless retarder beats any polarizer; its retardance grows with thickness, so the half-wave plate is a thickness (and a 45° axis) |
| 6-4 Stern–Gerlach | the magnet measures along its axis: align it with the packet's polarization to send everything up (cos² of the misalignment otherwise goes down onto a mine); the gradient aims the beam |
| 6-5 Bomb Tester | Kwiat's interaction-free measurement, unrolled: four 22.5° rotator plates, and stations whose V port is the bomb; one look per cycle beats doubling up, and the photon reaches the target having "seen" the bomb without touching it |
| 6-6 Humpty Dumpty | full-loop Stern–Gerlach interferometer in time: a gradient pulse splits H and V, and +τ, −2τ, +τ brings them back together at rest, so a 45° analyzer sees the coherent spin again (55 %, no recombination 0 %) |
| 7-1 Light Grating | Kapitza–Dirac: a flash of a standing light wave puts J_n(Vτ/2)² into momentum order n; the first orders peak at 65 % |
| 7-2 Self-Image | Talbot effect: a phase-grating flash turns into stripes that shift by half a period at T/2 and revive at T = d²/π; time the flash for a fixed observation |
| 7-3 Bragg Mirror | ridges lower than the packet's energy reflect it when k_in = π/a (59 %), and let it through otherwise |
| 8-1 Bunching | Hong–Ou–Mandel: ψ(x₁, x₂) of two identical bosons and a cross-shaped wall; at T = ½ they always leave on the same side (93 %, distinguishable 50 %) |
| 9-1 Loop Mirror | a Sagnac ring: a wall splits the packet, the halves go round in opposite senses and recombine; with no flux the ring is a mirror, a quarter flux quantum through its hole (Aharonov–Bohm) makes it a window (63 %, no flux 3 %) |
| 9-2 Push Less | Bloch oscillations in a ring-shaped lattice: holding the push brings the atom back every T_B = 2π/(Fa); letting go after about T_B/4 sends it gliding to the target (61 %, holding 8 %) |
| 9-3 Half Steps | on a ring only k = (2πn + θ)/L exist; a momentum target halfway between teeth is unreachable at any speed until half a flux quantum shifts the comb (62 %, no flux 0 %) |
| 9-4 Pairs | two particles on one ring (a torus): their total momentum shifts twice as fast with the flux, so a quarter flux quantum does what half did for one, and half does nothing: the pair flux quantum h/2e (42 % vs 0 %) |
| 9-5 Leak | the loop mirror with the right edge switchable into a drain: draining only the half that crosses it first lets the other half through the wall (interaction-free measurement, 20 % vs 6 %) |
| 9-6 Revival | a torus 10 wide: a packet dissolves and revives at T = L²/π; a flux moves where it revives (θL/π), so a quarter quantum brings it back half a ring away (77 % vs 0 %) |
| 9-7 Chopper | a velocity selector: the drain switched on early and late removes too-fast and too-slow atoms, and the score is the share of the surviving beam in the speed band (post-selected: 52 % vs 19 %) |
| 9-8 Bound Pair | two particles tied by a spring run round the loop mirror as one: the pair winds the flux twice, so its window opens at Φ₀/8 and Φ₀/4 (one particle's window) is a mirror: the h/2e of superconducting rings (85 % vs 16 %) |
| 9-9 Pauli Ring | two fermions on a ring cannot share k = 0; half a flux quantum shifts the comb to ±π/L and both fit in the cold square (parity effect: 100 % vs 0 %) |

3-1 has intrinsically small P_T (a slit screen and 2D diffraction spread the probability), so its star thresholds are low and it is exempt from the naive check; the Mach–Zehnder levels keep the probability in two beams and carry the interference lessons. See `docs/mechanics-review.md` for the review behind these levels and the roadmap.
